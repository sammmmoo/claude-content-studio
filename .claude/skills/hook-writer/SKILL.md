# hook-writer

**Outcome:** 4-6 hooks for one video, each a different idea, every one strong enough to film. The creator films 2-3 and the numbers pick the winner.

## Three hooks at once

Every video opens with three hooks working together from the very first frame:
- **Spoken:** the first line, said straight away (no "okay, so").
- **Text:** big, in the top third of the screen, there from frame 0. It names the topic plainly and can be worded differently from the spoken line.
- **Visual:** what you see at frame 0 shows what the video is about (a list board, a screenshot, a notification popping in).

All three must be plainly about the same topic. Clear beats clever: a teaser that hides the subject loses the viewer and confuses the algorithm about who to show it to.

## Shapes that keep winning (fill them in with the topic)

These are common hook patterns across short-form creators. Several are adapted from @usamatalks's "99 hooks" list, which is worth reading in full.

- **Steal this [thing]. It turns [input] into [result].**
- **Do this one thing [before X].**
- **This sounds stupid until you [try it / look at your own X].**
- **Don't [buy/do X] until you know this about [Y].**
- **If you only [do one thing about X], make it this.**
- **Nobody's going to tell you this about [X], so I will.**
- **What's the [best/fastest] way to [X]? Most people think [A] or [B]. It's neither.** (A and B must be what people really believe.)
- **[N] [things], worst to best. Only the last one [gets the result].**

Use each shape on a few videos, not every one, or your feed starts to repeat itself.

## Every hook must

- **Open a loop:** it makes them want the answer and holds the answer back. "It books a job that doesn't exist" gives it away. "Number three is the worst, because it sounds perfect" doesn't.
- **Be said in one breath:** about 10 words, how the creator talks.
- **Take a stance or name a stake:** not a newsreader opener.
- Never "by the end of this video", "I reckon", or a yes/no question.

## After the hook

- **Super hook:** one proof line the creator records once ("I've built over a hundred of these"). True only, and about this video's topic.
- **Stacks (write 3):** the line straight after the hook that makes them stay. Use one of three kinds: why listen to me ("I've tested every one of these"), a call-out ("And most of you are doing number three"), or an unexpected stake ("And the money's not even the worst bit"). "Saved the best for last" lines go here, not in the hook.
- **Lock-ins (write 3):** one line by second 8 that promises the payoff without listing the contents.

Check every hook with every stack and lock-in. If a hook already gave something away, a lock-in can't promise to reveal it. Any "it" or "that" must have been said out loud before it.

## Text hooks

Two per spoken hook, listed 1) and 2). Short, and each one stands on its own. One natural power word helps (simple, hidden, costly, overlooked), but never a formula tag like "THE SURPRISING PART". These go in the FOR THE EDIT block, never in the read copy.
